## Episode title

From Fast Runs to Honest Edge-Case Scoring

## Core thesis

The Million Claim Challenge is becoming more credible because it now separates speed from correctness, matched outcomes from mismatches, and unsupported validation gaps from true failures.

## Required discussion points

The hosts should explain:

1. Why Part 5 was about repeatable local Kubernetes throughput, while Part 6 is about correctness breadth.
2. Why a healthcare claims platform should not treat "paid" as the only successful outcome.
3. Why expected pends need to be observed through persisted claim status instead of guessed from synchronous adjudication responses.
4. Why unsupported scenarios are honest gaps, not wins and not platform failures.
5. How retro eligibility termination and newborn scenarios exposed fixture-quality issues.
6. How the 5K breadth run reached zero mismatches after PRs #853-#856.
7. Why the clean 10K run was a confidence gate, and how the 50K result became clean after closing the COB fixture-isolation mismatch.
8. Why 100K is intentionally deferred into its own later milestone instead of being added to Part 6.

## Important caveats

The hosts must say that:

- these are local Docker Desktop Kubernetes results, not production cloud results
- the local Docker Desktop allocation for the final 50K run was 18 CPUs and about 23.4 GiB memory
- the final 50K run used the long-lived local `demo` tenant, not a freshly reset tenant; raw seeding showed 49,425 members and 99,956 providers already present
- the run used seed 42, Medicaid line of business, prior-auth scenarios enabled at 2%, member seeding enabled, and provider seeding enabled
- the 5K run is a baseline, not the final Part 6 headline
- the first 50K attempt found one COB secondary-payer mismatch, which was fixed by isolating supported COB-pend fixture members before the clean rerun
- pend observation polls only expected-pend claims, so these results prove expected pends persisted as pended but do not yet include a false-pend sweep over expected-pay or expected-deny claims
- the `$59.36` average payment delta is the average absolute paid-claim difference between platform plan payment and the synthetic answer key; payment-amount accuracy is visible but is not yet part of the zero-mismatch workflow scoring gate
- 100K should be discussed only as a future focused milestone, not as a Part 6 requirement
- unsupported scenarios should not be oversold as validated correctness
- unsupported scenarios are concentrated in subrogation, prior-authorization edge variants, retro coverage change, behavioral health carve-out, and Medicaid spend-down
- the matched 50K footprint includes broader COB, Medicaid, retro eligibility, behavioral health, and newborn scenarios beyond the short headline list

## Tone

Make the conversation practical, candid, and engineering-led.

Avoid marketing hype. The strongest point is honesty: the benchmark got better because it became more willing to name gaps precisely.

## Suggested episode arc

1. Quick recap of Part 5: repeatable local Kubernetes benchmarking.
2. The next problem: throughput without correctness is not enough.
3. Explain pends and why persisted status matters.
4. Explain fixture bugs: retro dates, newborn age, authorization, duplicate line codes.
5. Walk through the 5K result.
6. Explain unsupported scenarios and why they are intentionally visible.
7. Close with the Part 6 plan: publish the clean 50K breadth result, then make 100K its own milestone.
