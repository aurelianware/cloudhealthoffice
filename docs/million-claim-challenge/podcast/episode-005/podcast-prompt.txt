# Episode 005 podcast prompt

Create a 10-12 minute conversational podcast episode titled:

**From Faster to Repeatably Faster**

Use the attached Episode 005 packet and the reusable host personas.

## Core story

CloudHealthOffice moved from isolated fast local runs to repeatable local Kubernetes benchmarking for the Million Claim Challenge.

The episode should explain how the team added a local Kubernetes sweep harness, compared parallelism settings, discovered that the best setting changed as workload size increased, found a hidden 10,000-claim cap, made the cap explicit with a max-claims override, and completed a true 50,000-claim run.

## Hosts

Use two hosts:

- Alex: systems architect focused on Kubernetes, performance, infrastructure, and repeatable benchmarking.
- Jordan: healthcare platform engineer focused on claims adjudication, benefit rules, prior authorization, CMS-0057-F, and workflow correctness.

## Required discussion points

- The local Kubernetes sweep harness.
- Configurable parallelism values.
- Repeated runs.
- Why one fast run is not a benchmark.
- The 1,000-claim sweep where p=11 looked best.
- The 10,000-claim sweep where p=10 became the better balance.
- The hidden 10,000-claim cap.
- The intentional max-claims override.
- The true 50,000-claim run.
- Why correct denials are not platform failures.
- Why throughput must be paired with workflow correctness.
- Why local Kubernetes is a proving ground, not a final production benchmark.

## Required benchmark numbers

1,000 claims, parallelism 11:

- 247.10 claims/sec
- 100.35 ms P95
- 128.53 ms P99
- 0 platform failures
- 160/160 workflow checks matched

10,000 claims:

- p=10: 209.54 claims/sec, 103.47 ms P95, 149.85 ms P99
- p=12: 209.81 claims/sec, 118.19 ms P95, 170.90 ms P99
- conclusion: p=10 is the better balance despite p=12 having a tiny throughput edge

50,000 claims:

- 50,000 requested
- 50,000 generated
- 50,000 processed
- 157.07 claims/sec
- 5 minutes 18 seconds elapsed
- 130 ms P95
- 175 ms P99
- 0 platform failures
- 4,000/4,000 workflow checks matched

Repeat 50,000-claim benchmark after provider seeding was skipped:

- 188.64 claims/sec
- 106 ms P95
- 151 ms P99
- 0 platform failures
- 4,000/4,000 workflow checks matched

## Tone and constraints

- Do not read the article verbatim.
- No marketing hype.
- Do not invent numbers, customers, or production claims.
- Explain Kubernetes Jobs, parallelism, P95/P99, and workflow checks clearly.
- Make the distinction between business denials and platform failures explicit.
- Include a recurring "Next Week's Challenge" ending.

## Suggested ending

Next Week's Challenge should point toward the next bottleneck: separating one-time setup costs from steady-state adjudication throughput, then moving toward larger local and cloud-backed validation runs.
