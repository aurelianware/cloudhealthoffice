The Health Plan Independence Manifesto

Health Plans Should Control Their Technology Future.

For too long, health plan technology strategies have been shaped by the constraints of proprietary administration systems.

Modernization becomes a negotiation over access, integration, licensing, and the right to change. The next generation of health plan administration should begin with a different premise:

Health plans deserve meaningful control over the technology that runs their businesses.

A standard for the next decision

Eight Principles for Meaningful Control.

01

Inspect the systems you depend on.

Source access makes a different conversation possible. Ask which components can be inspected, how evaluation is licensed and whether your security team can review the executing implementation. A diagram is useful; the ability to examine the logic behind it is stronger.

02

Make operational data accessible under explicit, enforceable terms.

Agree on schemas, APIs, retrieval formats, timing, fees and retention before they become exit negotiations. Test retrieval with representative data. Source visibility alone does not establish complete export capability or an enforceable right to retrieve everything.

03

Align infrastructure with your operating strategy.

Choose who controls the environment and who is accountable for backups, patching, identity, incident response and recovery. Customer-controlled infrastructure creates responsibilities as well as options. Put them in the operating agreement.

04

Make business rules understandable and auditable.

Ask how a benefit rule becomes an operational decision, who may change it and what evidence records the change. Inspectability should support review by operations, engineering and governance teams under applicable license terms.

05

Modernize incrementally, with evidence.

Define one boundary, one accountable owner and measurable acceptance criteria. Reconcile parallel results before cutover. A reversible plan requires tested rollback procedures, not simply a promise that migration will be easy.

06

Build AI readiness on accessible data and transparent workflows.

An AI feature cannot compensate for inaccessible records or unclear decision provenance. Require data-flow review, human approval boundaries, model evaluation and monitoring. Advisory suggestions should remain distinguishable from authorized operational decisions.

07

Make technology economics understandable.

Evaluate recurring PMPM, fixed costs, infrastructure, people, integration and exit obligations together. Lower recurring pricing can still produce a higher total cost. Use explicit assumptions and treat unknown commercial terms as unknown.

08

Choose vendor relationships that expand your options.

Commercial partners can supply expertise and continuity. Ask whether contracts, interfaces and operating practices leave your organization with meaningful alternatives. Negotiate change and exit procedures while both parties are planning success.

Commercial clarity

Independence Is Not the Absence of Commercial Relationships.

CloudHealthOffice is source-available under BSL 1.1. Production use requires a commercial license. Source availability does not transfer intellectual property or provide unrestricted production rights.

Support scope, operational obligations and service levels require an agreement. Data retrieval, retention, export validation, transition assistance and exit fees should be negotiated explicitly. Customer control is a practical arrangement between architecture, contracts and capable teams.
