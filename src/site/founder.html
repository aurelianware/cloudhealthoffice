The Founder

Built by a payer-systems practitioner, not a vendor.

Mark Phillips — Founder & Solution Architect, Cloud Health Office

Cloud Health Office exists because most payer software is sold by people who have never had to make a legacy core-admin system pass an audit at 2 a.m. I have. For years I've worked at the center of health-plan modernization programs — as the solution architect who understands the claims engine, the authorizations, the interoperability rules, and the cloud they all have to run on. This platform is that experience turned into software you can inspect.

Where the depth is

A rare combination in one practitioner

The value of a payer platform is only as real as the person who understands both the regulation and the plumbing. These are the areas I work in hands-on.

QNXT & legacy core-admin modernization

Deep, hands-on work with QNXT and the realities of Facets- and HealthEdge-class core administration — modernizing them without ripping them out, and knowing exactly which decisions are safe to make and which aren't.

Azure & cloud-native delivery

Significant experience bringing payer workloads — including QNXT — into Azure subscriptions the plan actually controls. The combination of core-admin depth and cloud architecture is hard to find in one person.

Claims, authorizations & payment integrity

The full claims lifecycle: adjudication, prior authorization, edits, and the payment-integrity decisions that determine whether a plan pays correctly — the substance behind every screen on this platform.

Interoperability & CMS-0057-F

A working command of interoperability and the CMS-0057-F rule — not as a slide, but as deadlines, APIs, and data flows a plan has to stand behind. The compliance work on this site reflects it.

Vendor accountability

I've served as the technical check on large implementation vendors — challenging estimates that aren't sound and keeping delivery honest. That posture is baked into how this platform is built and documented: evidence you can inspect.

Stakeholders & knowledge transfer

Translating fluently between business owners, IT staff, and engineering teams — and training internal staff so capability stays with the organization, not locked in a contractor's head.

Why I built Cloud Health Office

Every health plan I've worked with runs on a core-admin system that predates the regulations now being written for it. The pattern is always the same: a capable platform, an urgent compliance deadline, and a gap in the middle that gets filled with consulting hours instead of software. I kept solving the same problems — eligibility, claims, prior authorization, interoperability — one engagement at a time.

Cloud Health Office is those solutions, made durable. It's the compliance and modernization layer for legacy payer systems — meet CMS-0057-F without replacing QNXT, Facets, or HealthEdge — and it's built the way I run an implementation: evidence first, no hand-waving, every claim inspectable. That's why the site leads with a million-claim benchmark you can audit instead of a marketing number you have to trust.

“The people who understand payer systems deeply are rarely the people building the software for them. Cloud Health Office closes that gap — the platform is built by the practitioner, with the evidence in the open.”
