Scoping a QNXT-to-CMS-0057-F adapter.

The four required APIs — Patient Access, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization — are due January 1, 2027, and most of the data behind them already lives in QNXT. The hard part is getting it out cleanly.

CMS-0057-F is the federal rule that requires Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, and some exchange plans to offer FHIR APIs for patient access, provider access, payer-to-payer exchange, and prior authorization by January 1, 2027.

How we scope each adapter

No two QNXT installs are the same, so we scope one plan at a time.

QNXT version, hosting, licensed modules, and years of local configuration all change where the data sits and how safely it can be read. An adapter is scoping and build work for your environment, not a package we install. We find out what your environment actually holds before anyone prices it.

  1. Questionnaire

    Nine sections your QNXT, data, and compliance teams answer together. It tells us where each API's data lives and what has to be reconciled before it can be served.

  2. 90-minute discovery workshop

    We walk through the answers with your team, settle the open questions, and agree on sources, extraction approach, and test data.

  3. Fixed-scope SOW

    A statement of work with defined deliverables, assumptions, responsibilities, and exclusions, written from what the workshop established.

Phase 1 · Read path

Patient Access, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and metrics

Read QNXT member, claims, encounter, prior-authorization, and provider data and serve it through the read APIs. Prior-authorization metrics reporting is in this phase.

Phase 2 · Write-back

Prior Authorization write-back

Take prior-authorization requests arriving through the API back into QNXT. It depends on how authorizations enter QNXT today, so we scope it after the read path is understood.

What the questionnaire covers

Nine sections, from environment to sign-off.

A sample question or two from each section is below. The full questionnaire goes to your team when you request it.

  1. A Plan & QNXT environment

    • Which lines of business run on this QNXT instance?
    • Which QNXT version are you on, and is it hosted on-premises, Cognizant-hosted, or delivered as BPaaS?

  2. B Data access & extraction

    • Is a read replica or change data capture (CDC) feed available, or would extraction run against the production database?
    • Is claims and encounter history available back to 2016 dates of service, and where does it live if it has been archived?

  3. C Member & coverage

    • How do QNXT member identifiers crosswalk to the identifiers other payers and providers will present?
    • How are merged or duplicate member records handled, and is the merge history kept?

  4. D Claims & encounters

    • How are adjustment and reversal chains linked, and can the current version of a claim be identified reliably?
    • Where are cost-sharing amounts held, so they can be excluded from Provider Access responses?

  5. E Prior authorizations

    • Is each authorization's status history stored with timestamps, or only its current status?
    • Are specific denial reasons captured, and can drug authorizations, which are excluded, be separated out?

  6. F Providers, network & attribution

    • How is a provider's in-network status determined for a given date of service?
    • How are members attributed to providers, and where is that attribution kept?

  7. G Clinical data, consent & metrics

    • Where will member opt-in and opt-out choices be stored, and who maintains them?
    • How are prior-authorization metrics produced for reporting today?

  8. H PAS write-back (phase 2)

    • What path does an inbound X12 278 request take into QNXT today?
    • Where does the coverage and documentation policy content that CRD and DTR would present live today?

  9. I Security, testing & sign-off

    • What does your vendor security review require before a new integration can read QNXT data?
    • Will reconciliation between QNXT and the API layer be accepted as proof of completeness at sign-off?
