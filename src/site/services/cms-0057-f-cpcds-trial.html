CPCDS (the Common Payer Consumer Data Set) is the claims and coverage data set behind the CARIN Blue Button guide that CMS recommends for the Patient Access API. Most cores can already export something close to it, which makes it a practical way to get a plan's data out without a custom adapter.

The part worth trying first

Shadow adjudication: run your real claims through a second core, without switching.

Cloud Health Office can re-adjudicate the claims your core already decided and record, claim by claim and line by line, where its answer differs: allowed, paid, member liability and denial codes. Nothing a member, provider or API sees changes. No accumulator moves and no event is sent.

Payment integrity

Where the core may have paid too much or too little

The run report splits decided claims into “core paid more” and “core paid less”, in claims and dollars, and breaks the differences down by stage, denial code, procedure code and billing NPI. A discrepancy CSV goes to your payment-integrity team.

Migration rehearsal

How much of your volume a second core already handles

The undecided rate is the readiness number. For each claim it can't decide, the report names the missing input, such as no contract rate for the provider or a member not loaded. That list is the work plan.

Expect most claims to be undecided at first. Shadow adjudication needs the inputs your core uses: provider contracts and fee schedules (importers ship for contract rosters, fee schedule CSVs and the Medicare physician fee schedule by locality), benefit plan designs, and the provider network. Accumulators start at zero for now, so deductible differences show up as payment differences.

How it works

Getting your claims in: five steps, all with features that already ship.

The CMS-0057-F APIs are due January 1, 2027. Most of the data behind them already lives in your core. The trial shows whether that data, served through Cloud Health Office, passes the kits.

  1. Export from your core

    Members, coverages and claims as CPCDS files. Prior authorizations from your UM platform are optional, as CSV or X12 278.

  2. Dry run and map plan codes

    A dry run writes nothing. It lists what would be created, the core plan codes not yet mapped to a benefit plan, and the first errors in each file.

  3. Load

    Members and coverage first, then claims, then authorizations. Reloading the same files is safe: unchanged records are left alone.

  4. Validate with Inferno

    US Core, CARIN Blue Button and Plan-Net, run against the deployment with one of your loaded members and a member app's token.

  5. Keep it current

    Point the core's daily export at the drop folder. The Imports & EDI console shows each run, freshness and reconciliation, and every file is archived as received for ten years.

Questions plans ask

Frequently asked questions

Do we have to replace our core to use this?

No. Your core keeps adjudicating and stays the system of record. Cloud Health Office reads what the core exports and serves the CMS-0057-F APIs from it.

Which Inferno kits run against our own data?

US Core, CARIN Blue Button and Plan-Net. Bulk Data and PDex need the kit's SMART Backend Services key registered with your deployment first. Our own nightly results for all six kits, on synthetic data, are on the Inferno results page.

What about prior authorizations?

CPCDS has no prior authorizations, but the CMS-0057-F APIs and metrics need them. If your plan runs its own UM platform, load its decisions as CSV or X12 278 files, through the same folder. They are kept read-only and tagged with their source.

Can the daily drop replace the one-time load?

Yes. Once a load works by hand, the core writes the same files to the drop folder each day. Freshness and reconciliation against the core's control totals show in the portal.

What we do not claim

  • A passing kit is not certification and not a guarantee of regulatory compliance. There is no certification program for payer APIs under CMS-0057-F.
  • No named customers or production plan deployments are claimed on this site.
  • A kit tests one implementation guide at one version, in the groups that are run. Requirements no kit checks, such as decision timeframes, attribution and consent, need their own evidence.
  • Shadow adjudication results depend on the inputs loaded. They are findings to review, not a recovery estimate.
