Prior-authorization reporting evidence

10,000 prior authorizations, an answer key written first.

CMS-0057-F asks every impacted payer to decide prior authorizations within 72 hours or 7 days and to publish how it did. The PA Timeliness Challenge loads a synthetic year of requests whose right answers are computed from the rule before Cloud Health Office sees them, drives live requests through PAS, and checks every number the platform reports. On its first run on our Google Cloud demo, all 2,601 checks matched.

2,601 of 2,601 checks matched 10,000 requests · 4 lines of business GKE demo, November 3, 2026 Synthetic data · not an attestation

The PA Timeliness Challenge is the prior-authorization counterpart to our Million Claim Challenge. Both start from a corpus whose correct outcomes are known in advance and score what the deployed platform actually recorded.

The score

What was checked

The score is agreement, not a timeliness rate. The synthetic plan in the corpus decides about 87% of standard requests inside 7 days, on purpose, so that late decisions, extensions and appeals all appear in the reports. A pass means Cloud Health Office reported that plan's performance exactly as the rule says it should be counted.

History: the timing path

A real year of decisions can't be replayed in real time, so the history cohort is imported with its original receipt and decision times, the way a plan migrating from another system would load it. Extensions and appeals recorded in the source system come in as sourced facts. This phase exercises the reporting rules: deadlines, the 14-day extension, expedited versus standard, and what counts as decided.

Live: the workflow path

The live cohort goes in the way a provider would send it, as a Da Vinci PAS bundle. The challenge then acts as the reviewer: it takes the scripted extensions, records line-level decisions with denial reasons through the 278 response endpoint, and reads every request back. Live decisions happen within minutes, so this phase proves the workflow and the record, not the clock.

Written first

The corpus and its answer key

The generator writes a deterministic corpus from a seed: the same options give the same files, byte for byte. Each request is drawn from one of twelve scenarios, including decided at the deadline, decided one minute late, extended and decided inside the extension, extended and decided late, an extension asked for on a line of business that doesn't allow one, excluded drugs, undecided requests, and denials approved on appeal. Requests are spread across Commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and Exchange, and about a quarter are expedited.

The answer key is computed from the rule's 72-hour and 7-day timeframes and its one 14-day extension for Medicare Advantage and Medicaid. It doesn't call or copy any Cloud Health Office code. A preflight test runs the corpus through the real import and reporting code on every pull request, so a change that would make the reports disagree with the rule fails before it merges.

Stated up front

What the challenge found

Imported extensions were measured against the wrong deadline (fixed)

While the challenge was being built, its preflight test found that a Medicare Advantage or Medicaid request extended in its source system was counted as extended but still judged against the original 72-hour or 7-day deadline. A decision inside the extension counted as late. The fix moves the deadline only as the rule allows: on those lines of business, when the extension was granted by the original deadline, and by at most 14 days. It shipped before this run.

PAS has no Exchange code (open)

A PAS request carries its line of business as an X12 claim filing indicator, and that code set has no value for a marketplace plan. Live Exchange requests are therefore sent, and recorded, as Commercial. The live cohort is scored that way and the run says so. History requests, which carry their line of business directly, are reported as Exchange.

Reproducible

How to repeat it

The generator, the validator and the runner are in the public repository under src/tools/pa-timeliness-challenge. On the Google Cloud demo, the deploy workflow builds the challenge image beside the service images, and scripts/demo/gcp/run-pa-timeliness.sh runs it as a Kubernetes Job against the in-cluster services, then saves the job log and the run summary. Against any other stack, dotnet run --project src/tools/pa-timeliness-challenge -- run takes the authorization and FHIR service URLs and a bearer token for the run's tenant.

A rerun with the same seed is idempotent: the import reports the requests unchanged and the facts are keyed by event. The run checks the history period before it loads anything and warns if the tenant already holds other requests there, since the metrics count every request in the tenant.

Stated plainly

What this does not establish

Limitations

  • Synthetic data on a demo stack. No member data is involved, and no production deployment or production volume is claimed.
  • Not a CMS attestation. The numbers describe a synthetic plan. A plan's own report depends on its own decisions, its line-of-business mapping and its counsel's reading of the rule.
  • Agreement with our reading of the rule. The answer key encodes one interpretation of how CMS-0057-F counts decisions, extensions and appeals. Where the rule leaves room, a plan may count differently.
  • Prior authorization only. Patient Access, Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer are evidenced separately, by the Inferno kits and the acceptance scenarios. The challenge doesn't score the prior-authorization data a member sees through Patient Access.
  • One run. This is the first run on the demo. It isn't on a nightly schedule yet.
