A hypothetical example

Lower PMPM Can Still Mean Higher Total Cost.

At 100,000 members (1,200,000 member months), current recurring PMPM of $2 and alternative PMPM of $1.50, with $100,000 annual fixed costs each, current annual cost is $2.5 million and alternative annual cost is $1.9 million. These example recurring costs include hypothetical licensing and support assumptions; they are not a vendor quote.

Across three years, $2 million in transition/exit costs and $100,000 incremental parallel-run costs produce a current total of $7.5 million and alternative total of $7.8 million: $300,000 higher alternative cost, despite the lower recurring PMPM. Simple payback is 3.5 years, beyond the three-year horizon.

Method and limits

Make the Assumptions Reviewable.

V = annual member months; H = years; pA / pB = current / alternative PMPM; FA / FB = the sum of each scenario's annual fixed, infrastructure, support, staffing and integration costs; T = transition + exit; P = total incremental parallel-run costs.

  • Current annual cost A = V × pA + FA
  • Alternative annual cost B = V × pB + FB
  • Current horizon cost = H × A
  • Alternative horizon cost = H × B + T + P
  • Modeled difference = H × (A − B) − T − P
  • Effective alternative PMPM = (H × B + T + P) / (H × V)
  • Simple payback = (T + P) / (A − B), only when A − B is positive

This constant-volume, undiscounted model excludes taxes, inflation, financing, growth and the timing of cash flows. Simple payback is an approximation, especially when parallel-run expenses occur later. Validate assumptions with commercial and finance teams.
