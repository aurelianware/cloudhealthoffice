Cloud Health Office 6.1, 6.2 and What’s Coming in 6.3

The CMS-0057-F APIs finished and tested by Inferno, a way in for plans that keep their core, and a Texas and California Medicaid build.

Series: CMS-0057-F & Payer Modernization Published: November 6, 2026 For: Health plan IT, interoperability and Medicaid leads

On January 1, 2027, the Patient Access, Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer API requirements of CMS-0057-F take effect. Most health plans we talk to are not asking whether they need these APIs. They are asking two harder questions: how do we prove they work, and do we have to replace our core to get them?

The last three Cloud Health Office releases are our answer to both. Here is what shipped, what is coming next, and what is still open.

016.1 “Spartacus”: the CMS-0057-F APIs, finished

6.0 delivered the CMS-0057-F APIs. 6.1 closed most of what 6.0 listed as not yet done.

  • SMART Backend Services, both directions. A backend client now proves who it is with its own signed key, not a shared secret. Cloud Health Office does the same when it calls another payer for Payer-to-Payer. (“I’m Spartacus” is where the name came from.)
  • A public Provider Directory on Da Vinci Plan-Net 1.2.0, with every required search, chain and include.
  • Bulk Data $export completed, for providers, patients and Payer-to-Payer, including Da Vinci bulk member match.
  • CAQH CORE real-time eligibility (270/271) and claim status (276/277), plus dental claim (837D) intake that keeps tooth and oral-cavity data on the claim line.

The part we are proudest of is the boring part: the conformance tests. The first nightly runs of the Inferno test kits found real defects in our code. We fixed them in the open, and we published the results, failures included, before we published the passes.

026.2 “The Shining”: keep your core, get the APIs

Most plans are not going to rip out QNXT, Facets or HealthRules eight weeks before a federal deadline, and they shouldn’t have to. 6.2 is built for them.

  • A daily CPCDS file drop. A plan sends the CARIN CPCDS files its core already produces. Cloud Health Office loads members, coverage, claims, providers and prior authorizations, and serves Patient Access, Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer from them. It reports every day whether the data is current and adds up. On our hosted option the drop is a private bucket behind SSH-key-only SFTP, and every file is archived exactly as received.
  • Shadow adjudication. Cloud Health Office can re-adjudicate claims the core already paid, without touching anything a member sees, and compare the results line by line. It is a payment-integrity check and a migration rehearsal at the same time.
  • Payer-to-Payer, judged by an outside kit. The Inferno PDex payer-server kit now runs every night against our HRex $member-match. 13 of 13 tests pass.
  • DTR 2.2.0 $questionnaire-package, with CQL prepopulation, linked from CRD.
  • Benefit plans from public data. An importer for the CMS Exchange QHP files, and 25 state Medicaid and CHIP templates for Texas, California, Florida, Arizona and New York. Each value is cited to its source or marked “verify”. They are a starting point to check against the state contract, not a certified benefit design.

With 6.2, all five Inferno kits we ran passed with no failing tests, apart from a small set of documented issues in the kits themselves.

03Coming in 6.3: shadow in a day, and Texas and California Medicaid

Not released yet

6.3 is not tagged yet. The release notes are drafted, and these are the headline items. Details may change before release.

Shadow in a day

A plan drops its CPCDS files and, the same day, sees claim by claim where Cloud Health Office agrees with its core.

  • It is switched on per plan from the portal, without a redeploy.
  • It runs whole files in the background.
  • The report shows where the core paid more or less than Cloud Health Office would have, in claims and in dollars.
  • Every claim it could not decide says what is missing (no contract, member not loaded, no authorization) and which file or setting closes the gap.

A shadow run that says “undecided” without saying why is useless. One that hands you a load list is a project plan.

Texas and California Medicaid

Built for both states in parallel:

  • Encounters. 837P/837I encounter submission to HHSC through TMHP, and to DHCS, with 999 and 277CA reconciliation and a rework queue.
  • Medicaid pricing. APR-DRG and EAPG through the plan’s own licensed grouper, Texas and Medi-Cal DRG payment, FQHC/RHC, nursing facility per diem, and Texas psychiatric per diem with the IMD limit.
  • Third-party liability. Cost avoidance, the states’ pay-and-chase rules, and recovery cases on California’s 12-month clock.
  • Finance close. Capitation reconciled by member, month and rate cell, IBNR, the 42 CFR 438.8 Medicaid MLR, and 1099 totals.
  • State reports. Texas HHSC deliverables (appeals and complaints, MLR, claims lag, TPL/TPR) filled into HHSC’s own workbook, and the Medi-Cal grievance log on California’s clocks.

Also in 6.3

  • Six of six Inferno kits pass every night. The DTR payer-server kit joined US Core, CARIN BB, Plan-Net, Bulk Data and PDex.
  • DTR without a DTR app. The provider portal renders the plan’s documentation forms for practices whose EHR has no DTR client, and submits the prior authorization request that carries the answers.
  • The PA Timeliness Challenge. 10,000 synthetic prior authorization requests on our Google Cloud demo, and 2,601 of 2,601 timeliness checks matched.
  • Claims operations. Retro rate reprocessing after a fee schedule changes, with an impact report and approval-gated adjustments, plus claim entry, correction and reversal in the portal.

04What is still open

We would rather you hear this from us than find it in a pilot.

  • The Texas and California values marked “verify” (some rates, lookbacks and state file layouts) need checking against a plan’s own contract and the state’s current companion guides before real use.
  • Medicaid notice printing and mailing, PCP auto-assignment and network adequacy are next, not done.
  • System-level $export is not enabled, and there is no X12 278 bridge for FHIR prior authorization. We rely on HHS enforcement discretion there.
  • Almost everything new is off by default and is turned on per deployment or per plan.
  • Inferno results are from our seeded test profile and our demo environment, not from a plan’s production data.

05Why ship this fast

6.0, 6.1 and 6.2 all shipped between October 28 and October 31, and 6.3 is close behind. The deadline does not move, and each release carries its evidence with it: the test kit, the version, the run, and what passed. The release notes, the nightly Inferno results and the PA Timeliness Challenge are all public:
