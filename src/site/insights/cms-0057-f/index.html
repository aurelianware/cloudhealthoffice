About This Series

Interoperability without a rip-and-replace

CMS-0057-F asks impacted payers to stand up modern FHIR interoperability — including Prior Authorization API capabilities — while membership, benefits, provider, authorization, and claims processing keep running on existing systems. This series documents how Cloud Health Office implements those capabilities as real, running services: Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS wired to a terminology service and a prior authorization rule engine, deployed alongside a payer’s core — and, when the payer is ready, able to take over payer domains or serve as the core backend itself.

CMS-0057-F Readiness → The vendor-neutral readiness surface: APIs, Da Vinci IGs, and deployment model. Prior Authorization Guide → How prior authorization is configured and operated in the platform. Terminology Guide → FHIR-native CodeSystem, ValueSet, and ConceptMap/$translate capabilities. Architecture → How the services fit together across tenants and deployment targets.

How this connects to the CMS-0057-F Readiness page

The CMS-0057-F Readiness page is the product view: what the readiness surface covers and how it deploys. This Insights series is the engineering view: how CRD, DTR, PAS, terminology, and the prior authorization rule engine are actually implemented, and how a payer moves from “modernize around the core” to “CloudHealthOffice as the core” over time. Readiness in production still depends on a payer’s configuration, identity, source systems, policies, data quality, operations, security controls, governance, and legal and compliance review — installing the platform alone does not make a payer CMS-0057-F compliant.
