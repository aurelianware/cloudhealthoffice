Texas Medicaid Managed Care Appeals, Grievances & Provider Disputes

The clocks, levels and escalation paths a Texas MCO works to, for members and for providers, in one place, with every value cited to the rule or contract it comes from.

Last verified: October 17, 2026 By: Mark Phillips, Founder & Chief Architect For: Appeals, compliance, claims & provider relations leaders
Read this first

This is a working reference, not legal advice. Rules change and plan contracts add to them. Values marked To confirm come from secondary sources because the primary document couldn't be retrieved. Check your current Uniform Managed Care Contract (UMCC) and Uniform Managed Care Manual (UMCM) before you rely on any value.

At a glance

Texas STAR and CHIP members appeal to their MCO within 60 days of the notice. The MCO acknowledges in 5 business days and resolves in 30 days (72 hours if expedited), with up to 14 more days in limited cases. A member who loses has 120 days to request a State Fair Hearing and may also ask for an External Medical Review. Providers work through the MCO's complaint and claims-appeal process before going to HHSC (STAR) or the Texas Department of Insurance (CHIP).

01The level chain

MembersMCO appeal→State Fair Hearing+ External Medical Review
ProvidersMCO complaint or claims appeal→HHSC (STAR) · TDI (CHIP)

Medicaid members get one level of MCO appeal before the State Fair Hearing, as federal rules require (42 CFR 438.402). Provider disputes are set by Texas law and the MCO contract, not by the federal member rules.

03Notices

  • HHSC templates are required. Notice 1 (A, B, C) for adverse benefit determinations and Notice 2 (A, B, C) for internal appeal decisions, each a letter, flyer and form, in English and Spanish. The MCO fills bracketed fields and removes language that doesn't apply.
  • Plain language at a 4th to 6th grade reading level, unless HHSC prescribes the wording.
  • Reductions and terminations: mailed at least 15 business days before the change, which is stricter than the federal 10 days.
  • Final prior authorization decisions: to the member and the provider by the next business day. Provider notices may go by fax, portal or mail.
  • The mailing date is the postmark; without one, the meter date; without either, the date on the notice. Keep per-piece proof of mailing.

Source: UMCM 3.21 (version reviewed: v2.6, effective May 31, 2022).

A provider appealing for a member, with the member's written consent, is on the member track: member clocks, member notices, and no right to request continuation of benefits (42 CFR 438.402(c)(1)(ii)).

05Prompt pay

Measured from receipt of the claim (Gov't Code §540.0265):

  • Nursing facility, ICF or group home: 10 days.
  • Other long-term services and supports: 30 days.
  • Everything else: 45 days, or up to 60 days if the provider contract says so.

06Recoupment during an appeal

  • Fraud or abuse recoupment: the MCO may recoup only if the provider doesn't appeal or the final appeal decision goes the MCO's way, and must give written notice and offer informal resolution (1 TAC §353.1454(b)).
  • Missing electronic visit verification data: overpayments can't be recovered until the provider exhausts all appeal rights (Gov't Code §544.0503(b)).
  • Other overpayments: industry materials say providers may exhaust appeals before repayment on any claim. To confirm against the current UMCC.

Cloud Health Office treats these rules as a versioned rule pack per line of business and State: each level, clock and unit (calendar or business days), who may file, which notices are required, and whether recoupment must wait.
