Medicaid Managed Care Appeals, Grievances & Provider Disputes by State

Federal rules set the floor. States set the clocks plans are actually measured on, and they decide almost everything about provider disputes. Here are ten States side by side, every value cited.

Last verified: October 17, 2026 By: Mark Phillips, Founder & Chief Architect States: AZ · CA · CO · FL · IL · MI · NY · OH · OK · TX
Read this first

A working reference, not legal advice. Rules change, and each plan's State contract adds to them. Values marked To confirm rest on secondary sources. Check the current rule and your contract before relying on any value.

At a glance

Federal law gives Medicaid managed care plans at most 30 days to resolve a member appeal and 72 hours if expedited (42 CFR 438.408). Several States are stricter, down to 10 working days (Colorado) and 24 hours for expedited appeals (Illinois). Members then have 90 to 120 days to request a State fair hearing. Provider disputes are not covered by the federal member rules at all: each State sets its own path, from State agency review to binding arbitration.

01The federal floor

  • One plan appeal level, then a State fair hearing (42 CFR 438.402).
  • File within 60 days of the adverse benefit determination notice.
  • Resolve within 30 days (72 hours expedited), extendable by up to 14 days; a plan-initiated extension needs written notice within 2 calendar days (438.408(b)–(c)).
  • Miss a notice or timing requirement and the member is deemed to have exhausted the plan's process and can go straight to a fair hearing (438.408(c)(3)).
  • Fair hearing window: 90 to 120 days from the plan's decision, as the State chooses (438.408(f)).
  • Grievances: resolved within 90 days at most (438.408(b)(1)).
  • Providers: only on the member's behalf, with written consent and where State law allows, and they can't request continued benefits (438.402(c)(1)(ii)). A provider's own payment dispute is left to the State.

04What the differences mean for a plan

  • Units matter as much as numbers. Calendar days, business days and working days all appear. Ten working days in Colorado is not ten calendar days, and a holiday calendar has to be part of the clock.
  • Provider disputes can't run on member clocks. A system that applies 42 CFR 438.408 to a provider payment dispute gets the due date wrong and puts the case in the wrong audit report.
  • Recoupment has to check for an open dispute. In several States, collecting a contested overpayment is itself a violation.
  • Integrated plans are coming. Ohio's Next Generation MyCare (phased in during 2026) and Michigan's integrated D-SNPs (2026) need notices and appeals that cover Medicare and Medicaid rights together.
  • Rule text and contracts disagree. Arizona's rule says 30 days for a fair hearing request while its contract says 90; Colorado's rule says 120 while its member materials say 60. A plan needs both recorded and one enforced.

05Common questions

How long does a Medicaid managed care plan have to resolve a member appeal?

Federal rules allow at most 30 days, or 72 hours if expedited, with up to 14 more days in limited cases. Colorado (10 working days), Illinois (15 business days) and Ohio (15 calendar days) are stricter, and Florida (48 hours) and Illinois (24 hours) shorten expedited appeals.

How long does a member have to request a State fair hearing?

States choose 90 to 120 days from the plan's appeal decision. Most States here allow 120 days; Arizona's contract and Ohio allow 90.

Do the federal Medicaid appeal rules cover provider payment disputes?

No. They're a member system. A provider can use them only for a member, with written consent, where State law allows. A provider's own dispute follows State law and the plan contract.

Can a plan recoup an overpayment while the provider disputes it?

It depends on the State. California and Florida bar offsetting a contested overpayment until it's resolved; Ohio requires the plan to decide a timely dispute before recovering; Colorado holds recovery during a fee-for-service appeal; Oklahoma will hold disputed funds from 2027; Texas and Illinois restrict fraud-related recoveries. Federal Medicaid rules set no general hold.
