Accumulator Engine

Deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums and benefit limits, derived from finalized claims.

The accumulator engine tracks what each member and family has used of a benefit plan year: deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, visit counts, day counts and dollar limits. The benefit calculation step of claims adjudication reads these balances to compute cost sharing, and writes them back once a claim is adjudicated.

Design: balances are derived, not stored

The source of truth for an accumulator is the finalized claim history, not a balance table. Cloud Health Office calculates balances from approved, partially paid and paid claim lines and keeps the result in a Redis hot cache so adjudication stays inside its latency budget. This mirrors how QNXT derives AccumBalance from claim lines, without an update-and-retry loop on a balance row.

  • No double counting under concurrency. Writes are atomic on the cache server, so two claims for the same member adjudicating at once cannot both read a stale balance.
  • Exact money. Amounts are stored as integer cents. A limit reached exactly reads as met, and an amount with precision below one cent is rejected rather than rounded, because it means pricing upstream is wrong.
  • Self-healing cache. If a balance is missing or the cache is flushed, the next read rebuilds it from claim history. No data is lost.
  • Plan-year reset without a batch job. Cached balances expire 14 months after they are written, which covers the plan year plus a run-out period for late claims.

Accumulator types and network tiers

Balances are kept for the individual member and for the family (subscriber plus all dependents), per plan and plan year, and split by network tier.

TypeWhat it tracks
Individual deductiblePer-member deductible applied year to date
Individual out-of-pocket maximumPer-member out-of-pocket spend year to date
Family deductibleAggregate deductible across all enrolled family members
Family out-of-pocket maximumAggregate family out-of-pocket spend
Visit countVisits counted per service type code (for example, service type 98 for office visits)
Day countInpatient or facility days, such as skilled nursing facility days
Dollar limitDollar-based benefit maximums, such as an annual chiropractic limit

Network tiers are in-network, out-of-network, tier 1 and tier 2.

Claim lifecycle

Adjudication

After cost sharing is calculated, the adjudication workflow applies the claim’s accumulator updates for the individual and family scopes in one atomic operation per scope. The claims pipeline journals each claim’s contribution, so a re-commit of the same claim or a replacement claim can undo exactly what was applied before.

Cache miss and rebuild

When a balance is not in the cache, the engine asks the claims service for accumulator totals for that member or family, plan and plan year, and writes the answer back. If the claims service cannot answer, nothing is cached and the next read tries again. A rebuild that races a concurrent claim is retried, so a stale answer is never cached.

Voids and reversals

When a claim is voided or reversed, both the individual and family balances are invalidated. The next read rebuilds them from claim history, which no longer includes the reversed claim. This is correct whether or not the balance was cached.

Audit trail

When the audit writer is enabled, every accumulator change is recorded durably before the operation returns. If the audit write fails, the operation fails and the caller retries it; a lost audit row is never silently accepted. Audited operations are safe to repeat, and the writer is idempotent per claim. The audit trail backs member-facing displays (“you’ve met $750 of your $1,500 deductible”), compliance reporting and investigation, and it is not touched when the cache is rebuilt.

Current limit

The engine keeps plan-year balances. Point-in-time historical accumulation (what a balance was on a past date) is not implemented. See the capability ledger for current status.
